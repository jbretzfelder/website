    Levels of Care quick reference

    The continuum, the litmus test, and the five domains — one page, meant to sit open next to a chart while you're actually weighing a transition, not read start to finish.

    Before anything else: the litmus test — can this person maintain their recovery gains for 12–16 hours without professional oversight? If no, they need more structure. If yes across every domain, they're ready for less.

    The continuum, low to high

    0.5 — Early Intervention: Education and monitoring for at-risk use, not yet a diagnosable disorder.
    1 — Outpatient: Scheduled sessions, client living independently full-time.
    2.1 — Intensive Outpatient (IOP): Several hours of programming, several days a week, living independently.
    2.5 — Partial Hospitalization (Day Treatment): 20+ hours/week programming, evenings and nights at home.
    3.1 — Clinically Managed Low-Intensity Residential: Live-in, lighter clinical structure, often longer-term.
    3.5 — Clinically Managed High-Intensity Residential: Live-in, 24/7 clinical (non-medical) oversight, no nursing on site.
    3.7 / 3.7WM — Medically Monitored Intensive Inpatient / Withdrawal Management: Live-in, 24/7 medical monitoring, nursing and physician availability.
    4 — Medically Managed Intensive Inpatient: Hospital-level care for the most acute medical/psychiatric presentations.

    3.7WM / 3.5 / 2.5 — the job, not just the intensity

    • 3.7WM: Medical stabilization. Nursing always available.
    • 3.5: Skill-building and behavioral change. No nursing on site — clinical staff only.
    • 2.5: Real-world application. Evenings at home; the point is proving skills hold up with less supervision.

    Five domains for a 3.5 → 2.5 decision

    • Medical Complexity — independent medication management, recognizing emergencies, communicating with providers
    • Psychiatric Stability — managing symptoms without 24/7 oversight, using coping skills consistently, recognizing warning signs
    • Social Skills — navigating relationships and conflict without staff mediation
    • Daily Living Skills — cooking, cleaning, budgeting, time management, transportation
    • Support Systems — recovery-oriented relationships, appropriately engaged family/social supports, accessible community resources
    One weak domain is enough to hold someone at the higher level of care — even when every other domain looks ready. That's not overcaution; a single unresolved risk area is exactly what a relapse exploits.
