    Co-occurring disorders quick reference

    The scope test, the withdrawal caveat, and the suicidality protocol — one page, meant to sit open next to you in the moment, not read start to finish. This does not replace the official policy — see "Handling a Client with Suicidal/Homicidal Ideation/Intent" for the full procedure.

    The scope test: we're always treating mental health, alongside substance use, as long as the substance use concern is still the bigger of the two. The moment mental health symptoms supersede the substance use concern — the client can no longer safely or meaningfully engage here because of them — that's a level-of-care conversation, not a "manage it and move on" situation. When in doubt, consult your supervisor.

    Withdrawal vs. diagnosis

    • Early recovery is not a reliable window for diagnosing anything — withdrawal can closely mimic anxiety, depression, psychosis, and mood instability
    • Don't diagnose off of week one. Document what you observe, not a conclusion about what it means
    • Establish and track a baseline once withdrawal clears — that baseline, not day one, is what you compare against later

    Stability, not symptom-free

    • The goal is a client who can hold their baseline, even an imperfect one — not a client with zero symptoms
    • Ask: can they engage in group and individual sessions, even minimally? That's the functional question that matters most
    • Decompensation — a real drop below baseline, not just a bad day — is what triggers a level-of-care conversation with your supervisor

    Suicidal/homicidal ideation vs. intent

    Ideation: thoughts of harming self/others, no stated intent. → Lethality assessment/suicide screener + safety plan if clinically appropriate. Consult Clinical Director/Clinical Supervisor if exclusionary criteria could be met.
    Intent: client expresses they will act on it, in that moment. → Immediately contact clinical/medical staff or supervisor on duty. Do not leave the client alone, including in the bathroom. No safety plan is written when intent is present.
    • Asking directly ("Are you having thoughts of hurting yourself?") does not increase risk — it opens the door
    • A disclosure is not attention-seeking. Treat it as real every time
    • Full steps, contacts, and documentation requirements are in the official Crisis Intervention procedure — this card is a memory jog, not the policy itself
