    Cultural humility quick reference

    The definition, what counts as culture, and the core habits of mind — one page, meant to sit open before a session where difference is showing up, not read start to finish.

    The core reframe: you cannot know someone's story by knowing their category. The client in front of you is always the leading expert on their own life — your job is to stay curious, not to already know.

    Cultural humility vs. cultural competence

    • Cultural competence implies a body of knowledge you can finish learning.
    • Cultural humility is a lifelong stance of curiosity and self-reflection — never complete, never a credential you earn once.

    What counts as "culture"

    All of it shapes how someone sees the world, help, and recovery — no single one defines a person.

    Race & ethnicity
    Income & class
    Geography (rural/urban)
    Religion & spirituality
    Political views
    Gender
    Sexual orientation
    Disability
    Age & generation
    Language & immigration history
    Family structure
    Military/veteran status

    Intersectionality

    • Overlapping identities interact — they don't just stack up one at a time.
    • Two people who share one identity can have completely different experiences once you factor in the rest of who they are.

    Practical habits

    • Ask open, respectful questions instead of guessing from appearance or category — "What's important for me to understand about your background?"
    • Notice your own reaction or assumption, name it to yourself, and set it aside rather than acting on it
    • Political views, like faith, are something to stay curious and neutral about — never something to share, debate, or judge in session
    • When you get something wrong, a brief, genuine correction and moving on works better than an extended apology that centers your discomfort
