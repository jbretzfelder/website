    ASAM quick reference

    One page, all six dimensions, the rating scale, and the core reminders — meant to be open next to Remarkable, alongside DocAssist, while you're actually writing, not read start to finish.

    Before anything else: plain language beats clinical-sounding language, every time. And you're not being asked to already know the answer — you're licensed to exercise judgment. Every dimension below is really just: what did I observe, and what does my judgment say it means for this level of care?

    The six dimensions, one line each

    1 — Acute Intoxication/Withdrawal: Not "any symptoms right now?" but "how resolved is this, and is anyone still watching it?" Don't write "N/A" — document the residual gap between length of use and length of abstinence.
    2 — Biomedical Conditions: Does a physical condition raise relapse risk or change treatment needs? If yes, say exactly how — don't leave it as background history.
    3 — Emotional/Behavioral/Cognitive: Report what you observed (irritability, avoidance, tearfulness) — you don't need a diagnosis to document a behavior.
    4 — Readiness to Change: Not "is she motivated" but "does stated intent match actual behavior?" Name the gap, don't just report the motivation.
    5 — Relapse/Continued Use Potential: Not "how's she doing here" but "what happens without this level of care?" Engagement belongs in Dimension 4, not here.
    6 — Recovery Environment: Not "does the family love her" but "does the environment have structural boundaries against use?" Those are different questions.

    Rating scale

    • 0 — No signs or symptoms in this dimension
    • 1 — Mild; unlikely to interfere with treatment
    • 2 — Moderate; requires monitoring, some interference likely
    • 3 — Significant impairment; requires active intervention at this LOC
    • 4 — Severe/imminent; requires the highest available structure

    Every sentence, three tests

    • Specific: does it have a number, date, quote, or observed behavior — or could it describe any client on any day?
    • Evidence, not opinion: could a reviewer picture it, or do they have to take your word for it?
    • Thread-connected: does it trace back to something in the ASAM, and forward to a goal or a note?

    Common mistakes, all six dimensions

    Every pitfall from every dimension deep dive, at a glance.

    Dimension 1
    Writing "N/A" and stopping
    Document the gap between length of use and length of abstinence, even with zero acute symptoms.
    Dimension 2
    Treating a condition as background history
    If it connects to relapse risk, say so directly — it's part of the clinical picture, not a footnote.
    Dimension 3
    Withholding observations because nothing's diagnosed
    No diagnosis needed to report irritability, avoidance, or tearfulness. Report what you saw.
    Dimension 4
    Unqualified "good insight and motivation"
    True and unqualified, it's a reason to discharge. Describe the gap between words and actions.
    Dimension 5
    Describing engagement instead of relapse risk
    Doing well here vs. managing without here are different questions. Engagement belongs in Dimension 4.
    Dimension 6
    Crediting a "supportive family" without addressing safety
    Loving and safe are different questions. Describe the actual structural boundaries against use.
    Every dimension
    Genericness
    If a sentence would still be true with the client's name swapped out, add the number, date, quote, or behavior that makes it theirs.

    Frequently asked questions

    The questions staff actually ask, answered directly. Click each one.

    + What if I'm not sure this is the right rating?
    You're not expected to be certain — you're licensed to exercise judgment. Pick the rating you can defend with the specific evidence you observed, and write that evidence down. A defensible 2 beats a guessed 3 every time.
    + What if nothing notable happened with this client this week?
    "Nothing notable" is itself information, if you say what it means. "Client continued to attend all groups without incident, and reported no new cravings this week" is a real sentence — it just needs the same specificity as any other week.
    + What if I use the wrong clinical term?
    Plain language that's accurate will always beat clinical-sounding language that's vague. Say what you saw or heard in your own words — that's the actual job.
    + What if the client barely talked in session?
    Limited engagement is data too. Document what you asked, what (if anything) they offered, and what you observed about their affect or willingness to engage — that's still a real clinical picture of the week.
    + What if something changes after I've already documented it?
    You're documenting your judgment at the time, based on what you knew then — not predicting the future. If something changes, the next note or the next Continued Stay ASAM is where that update belongs.
    + What does "Level of Care" actually mean?
    The intensity of treatment a client is currently receiving — for example, 3.5 Residential. Almost everything in an ASAM exists to justify why the client needs to stay at this intensity, or supports stepping them down to a less intensive one.
