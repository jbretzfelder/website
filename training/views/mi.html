    Motivational interviewing quick reference

    The spirit, the stages, OARS, and the change-talk categories — one page, meant to sit open next to you before a hard conversation, not read start to finish.

    The core reframe: motivation isn't a fixed trait a client either has or doesn't — it's a state that fluctuates by the hour, and your job in nearly every interaction is to protect and grow it, not test for it.

    The MI spirit — PACE

    Partnership: you and the client work side by side. You're not the expert fixing them.
    Acceptance: absolute worth, accurate empathy, autonomy support, affirmation — all four, all the time.
    Compassion: acting in the client's best interest, not your own relief or convenience.
    Evocation: the reasons to change already live in the client. Your job is to draw them out, not install them.

    Stages of Change

    • Precontemplation — not seeing a problem, or not yet. Goal: raise doubt, don't argue.
    • Contemplation — ambivalent. Sees both sides. Goal: tip the balance, explore both sides honestly.
    • Preparation — decided, not yet acting. Goal: help build a concrete plan.
    • Action — actively doing the work. Goal: support and reinforce.
    • Maintenance — sustaining the change. Goal: prevent relapse, plan for triggers.

    Most clients arrive in precontemplation or contemplation, often pushed here by someone else's ultimatum, not their own resolve. Meet them where they actually are, not where you wish they were.

    OARS

    Open questions: can't be answered in one word. "What worries you about your use?" not "Are you worried about your use?"
    Affirmations: name a genuine strength or effort. Specific, not generic praise.
    Reflections: state back what you heard, including the meaning underneath it. Aim for more reflections than questions.
    Summaries: collect several threads together, often ending on the change-talk thread.

    Change talk — DARN-CAT

    • D-A-R-N (preparatory): Desire ("I want to"), Ability ("I could"), Reason ("because"), Need ("I have to")
    • C-A-T (mobilizing): Commitment ("I will"), Activation ("I'm ready to"), Taking Steps ("I already did")

    The righting reflex — catch yourself doing this

    • Arguing for change while the client argues the other side — it recruits them straight into defending the status quo
    • Lecturing, warning, or listing reasons to change before they've asked
    • Confronting denial head-on instead of rolling with it
    • Playing the expert who has the answer, instead of drawing the answer out of the client
