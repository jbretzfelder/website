    Group facilitation quick reference

    Group types, the working group, the facilitator toolkit, and blocking scripts — one page, meant to sit open before you walk into group, not read start to finish.

    The core reframe: addiction thrives in isolation. Group is where connection — the thing addiction works hardest to cut off — gets rebuilt, on purpose, every day.

    Two kinds of group

    • Process groups: process what clients are feeling and going through right now. Sharing lets clients relate their struggle to others' and realize they're not alone.
    • Recovery support groups: psychoeducational — skills, tools, and recovery content, more structured and curriculum-driven.

    The working group vs. the four ways it slips

    • Working group: on-task, present, members turning toward each other and the material, even when it's hard.
    • Dependency: group looks to you to fix everything instead of working itself.
    • Fight-flight: conflict or distraction that avoids the real topic.
    • Pairing: two members carry a side conversation that quietly excludes the group.
    • Storytelling/monopolizing: group settles into a passive audience for one member's narrative instead of working together.

    The facilitator toolkit

    Opening: set the container — purpose, guidelines, tone — before content starts.
    Linking: connect one member's share to another's experience out loud.
    Evoking: draw out quieter members without putting them on the spot.
    Blocking: interrupt unhelpful behavior (advice-giving, cross-talk, gossip) without shaming the member.
    Redirecting: bring a wandering group back to the group's actual work.
    Closing: end with a deliberate wrap, not just running out of time.

    Blocking scripts

    • Advice-giving: "Let's hold advice for a moment — can you reflect back what you're hearing instead?"
    • Cross-talk/side conversation: "Let's bring that back to the whole group — what were you two talking about?"
    • Storytelling/monopolizing: "I want to pause you there so we can hear from some others — we'll come back if there's time."
    • Gossip about a peer: "Let's keep the focus on your own experience of that, not [peer]'s."
