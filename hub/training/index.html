      ASAM quick reference

      One page, all six dimensions, the rating scale, and the core reminders — meant to be open next to Remarkable, alongside DocAssist, while you're actually writing, not read start to finish.

      Before anything else: plain language beats clinical-sounding language, every time. And you're not being asked to already know the answer — you're licensed to exercise judgment. Every dimension below is really just: what did I observe, and what does my judgment say it means for this level of care?

      The six dimensions, one line each

      1 — Acute Intoxication/Withdrawal: Not "any symptoms right now?" but "how resolved is this, and is anyone still watching it?" Don't write "N/A" — document the residual gap between length of use and length of abstinence.
      2 — Biomedical Conditions: Does a physical condition raise relapse risk or change treatment needs? If yes, say exactly how — don't leave it as background history.
      3 — Emotional/Behavioral/Cognitive: Report what you observed (irritability, avoidance, tearfulness) — you don't need a diagnosis to document a behavior.
      4 — Readiness to Change: Not "is she motivated" but "does stated intent match actual behavior?" Name the gap, don't just report the motivation.
      5 — Relapse/Continued Use Potential: Not "how's she doing here" but "what happens without this level of care?" Engagement belongs in Dimension 4, not here.
      6 — Recovery Environment: Not "does the family love her" but "does the environment have structural boundaries against use?" Those are different questions.

      Rating scale

      • 0 — No signs or symptoms in this dimension
      • 1 — Mild; unlikely to interfere with treatment
      • 2 — Moderate; requires monitoring, some interference likely
      • 3 — Significant impairment; requires active intervention at this LOC
      • 4 — Severe/imminent; requires the highest available structure

      Every sentence, three tests

      • Specific: does it have a number, date, quote, or observed behavior — or could it describe any client on any day?
      • Evidence, not opinion: could a reviewer picture it, or do they have to take your word for it?
      • Thread-connected: does it trace back to something in the ASAM, and forward to a goal or a note?

      Common mistakes, all six dimensions

      Every pitfall from every dimension deep dive, at a glance.

      Dimension 1
      Writing "N/A" and stopping
      Document the gap between length of use and length of abstinence, even with zero acute symptoms.
      Dimension 2
      Treating a condition as background history
      If it connects to relapse risk, say so directly — it's part of the clinical picture, not a footnote.
      Dimension 3
      Withholding observations because nothing's diagnosed
      No diagnosis needed to report irritability, avoidance, or tearfulness. Report what you saw.
      Dimension 4
      Unqualified "good insight and motivation"
      True and unqualified, it's a reason to discharge. Describe the gap between words and actions.
      Dimension 5
      Describing engagement instead of relapse risk
      Doing well here vs. managing without here are different questions. Engagement belongs in Dimension 4.
      Dimension 6
      Crediting a "supportive family" without addressing safety
      Loving and safe are different questions. Describe the actual structural boundaries against use.
      Every dimension
      Genericness
      If a sentence would still be true with the client's name swapped out, add the number, date, quote, or behavior that makes it theirs.

      Frequently asked questions

      The questions staff actually ask, answered directly. Click each one.

      + What if I'm not sure this is the right rating?
      You're not expected to be certain — you're licensed to exercise judgment. Pick the rating you can defend with the specific evidence you observed, and write that evidence down. A defensible 2 beats a guessed 3 every time.
      + What if nothing notable happened with this client this week?
      "Nothing notable" is itself information, if you say what it means. "Client continued to attend all groups without incident, and reported no new cravings this week" is a real sentence — it just needs the same specificity as any other week.
      + What if I use the wrong clinical term?
      Plain language that's accurate will always beat clinical-sounding language that's vague. Say what you saw or heard in your own words — that's the actual job.
      + What if the client barely talked in session?
      Limited engagement is data too. Document what you asked, what (if anything) they offered, and what you observed about their affect or willingness to engage — that's still a real clinical picture of the week.
      + What if something changes after I've already documented it?
      You're documenting your judgment at the time, based on what you knew then — not predicting the future. If something changes, the next note or the next Continued Stay ASAM is where that update belongs.
      + What does "Level of Care" actually mean?
      The intensity of treatment a client is currently receiving — for example, 3.5 Residential. Almost everything in an ASAM exists to justify why the client needs to stay at this intensity, or supports stepping them down to a less intensive one.

        ITP quick reference

        One page — the anatomy, the numbering system, and the core reminders — meant to be open next to Remarkable, alongside DocAssist, while you're actually writing an ITP, not read start to finish.

        Before anything else: if you can't point to the ASAM sentence that justifies a goal, the goal doesn't belong in the plan yet. Every goal, objective, and intervention should trace back to something already documented.

        Three sections, matched to dimension pairs

        Section A — Dimensions 1 & 2 (e.g., Substance Use Disorders, Opioid Dependence, Chronic Pain)
        Section B — Dimensions 3 & 4 (e.g., PTSD, Anxiety, Depression, Family Conflicts, Treatment Resistance, and many more)
        Section C — Dimensions 5 & 6 (Relapse Proneness, Living Environment Deficiency, Parent-Child/Partner Relational Problems, Peer Group Negativity), plus an optional Case Management goal block
        Letters (A, B, C...) reset at the start of every section. A goal's real identity is Section + Letter together — "Section C, Goal A" is not the same goal as "Section A, Goal A."

        The three layers

        Goal: Picked from a category's preset options and edited, or written from scratch as a custom goal — never left as unedited library text. Gets a letter within its section. Never "completed" — it's a direction, not a checkbox. Requires a direct client quote. Carries one date: Established (auto-filled to today at generation).
        Objective: Checked off from the category's preset pairs, or added custom when nothing standard fits. Numbered to its goal's letter (1A, 2A, 1B...). Carries two dates: Start Date (auto-filled to today) and Target Date (the one field you actually choose).
        Intervention: Paired automatically with its objective when checked. Shares the objective's number/letter (Intervention 1A). Describes clinician action, not client behavior.

        Every goal, three tests

        • Traceable: does it answer a specific gap named in the ASAM, or could you not point to where it came from?
        • Individualized: has it been edited from the library default, and does it have a client quote attached?
        • Measurable: does the objective have a real Target Date (not "TBD") and a condition a reviewer could check off as done or not done?

        Quick gut-checks

        • If the objective could be true forever and never "complete," it's a goal wearing an objective's number.
        • If the intervention describes what the client will do, rewrite it as what the clinician will do.
        • Not every ASAM dimension needs a goal — write one where there's an active, unresolved need driving the current level of care.
        • DocAssist doesn't track review history — Established and Start Date just reflect the day you generate the plan. At review time, update the Target Date and document progress in the record yourself; the tool won't do it for you.

        Common ITP mistakes, at a glance

        Every pitfall from this module, gathered in one place.

        Anatomy
        Writing a goal where an objective belongs
        If it has no target date and could be true forever, it's a restated goal — not a measurable step.
        Sections
        Forgetting letters reset per section
        "Goal A" alone is ambiguous — always say which section. Section B's Goal A and Section C's Goal A are different goals.
        Objectives
        Leaving the Target Date at "TBD"
        "TBD" is really no target at all. Set a real date — even "next treatment plan review" works if that's a defined checkpoint.
        Interventions
        Describing what the client will do, not the clinician
        An intervention is your action — the technique or modality you'll use. The client's action belongs in the objective.
        Individualizing
        Accepting the library goal unedited, with no client quote
        This is exactly what triggers DocAssist's SMART warning and quote warning. Edit the text, and add her own words.
        Goal count
        A goal for every dimension, whether it needs one or not
        Write goals where there's an active, unresolved need — not as a box-checking exercise across all six dimensions.
        Reviews
        Assuming DocAssist tracks review history for you
        It doesn't — Established and Start Date just reflect the day you last generated the plan. If you regenerate a goal at a later review, its Established date will show that later day, not the original. Keeping track of history is on you and the record, not the tool.

        Frequently asked questions

        The questions staff actually ask about writing ITPs, answered directly. Click each one.

        + What if a client has a real need, but I'm not sure it rises to a whole goal?
        Not every true statement about a client needs its own goal — that's what the rest of the ASAM narrative is for. Ask: is this actively driving the need for this level of care right now? If yes, it earns a goal. If it's stable or resolving on its own, it may just need continued monitoring noted in the ASAM.
        + Do I have to use one of the library's goal categories?
        No. Every section has a "+ Add Custom Goal" option, letting you write the goal name, text, objectives, and interventions entirely from scratch. This isn't a workaround for edge cases — it's just as normal a path as the library, and often the right call when a client's situation doesn't map cleanly onto a preset category.
        + Can I just use one of the library's preset goal options as-is?
        You can start there, but it has to be edited before it's ready — DocAssist will flag a goal left exactly as the library wrote it with a SMART warning before you can submit. The library gives you a clinically sound starting point; making it true of this specific client is still your job.
        + Why does every goal need a client quote?
        It documents collaborative treatment planning — that the goal was built with the client, not just written about her. DocAssist will flag a goal that's missing one before you can submit.
        + What if none of the preset objective/intervention pairs fit?
        Add a custom one. This is a normal, expected part of the process — the library covers common ground, but a client's specific circumstances (like a specific person to avoid calling) sometimes need a pair written just for them.
        + What Target Date should I use if I'm not sure how long something will take?
        Pick the next treatment plan review date, or another real, defined checkpoint. Don't leave it at "TBD" — that's not a target, it's the absence of one.
        + If I regenerate an existing goal at a later review, does DocAssist remember when it was first written?
        No. Established and Start Date are always auto-filled to today, whenever you generate the output. If you regenerate a goal at a later review, its Established date will show that later day, not the original — so document the original date elsewhere in the record if it matters.
        + Do I need a separate goal for every ASAM dimension that has any rating above 0?
        No. A dimension can be actively monitored without a dedicated ITP goal if it isn't the thing driving continued need for this level of care. Goals belong to the findings doing the most clinical work.
        + Why is "Goal A" in Section B a different goal from "Goal A" in Section C?
        Letters reset at the start of every section, since each section is scoped to its own pair of dimensions. A goal's real identity is the section and the letter together — always reference both.
        + Who actually writes the intervention — me, or the client?
        You. The intervention is always the clinician's action — the specific technique or modality you'll use. What the client will do in response is the objective, one layer up.

          Individual Note quick reference

          One page — the two output boxes, the reference format, the four session types, and the risk line — meant to be open next to Remarkable, alongside DocAssist, while you're actually writing a note.

          Before anything else: a progress note is not a therapy note. Document progress toward the objective — not a transcript of everything discussed. Overly detailed personal disclosures can be subpoenaed and used against the client; they don't belong here.
          Second reminder: if a note doesn't reference a specific goal letter and objective number, it isn't documentation of treatment toward a plan — it's just a record that a conversation happened.

          Progress note, not therapy note

          • Ask: does this document progress toward the objective, or does it just repeat what she told me? If it's the second, leave it out.
          • Acknowledge clinical relevance without transcribing the disclosure — "client identified a trigger connected to a past event" not the details of the event itself.
          • This record can be subpoenaed. Anything written here beyond what's needed to show progress can end up used against the client, not for them.

          Two output boxes

          Describe Therapeutic Interventions Provided: what objective(s) you worked on and what you did in session — built from your Session Description plus auto-generated lines about which objectives were reviewed or assigned.
          Response — Intervention/Progress/Clinical Judgement: how the client responded, whether reviewed objectives were completed, what's next, and the risk & safety line — every note gets one, even if nothing is wrong.

          The reference format

          • Objective reference = Section + Goal Letter + Objective Number: "Objective 1A of Goal A of Section C" — the section is now included, so a bare "Goal A" no longer has to be disambiguated from memory.
          • It's still worth a glance that the section named actually matches where that goal lives, especially with similarly-named goals across sections.
          • "Objective(s) Reviewed" rows can be marked Completed; "Objective(s) Assigned Next" rows cannot — they haven't happened yet.

          Four session types

          • Working off ITP (Reviewing) — the default, routine session type.
          • Updating/Revising ITP — when the plan itself is changing, not just being worked.
          • First Meeting/Initial ITP — no "reviewed" objectives yet; only "assigned next."
          • Crisis Intervention — hides ITP reference fields entirely; today's note isn't measured against the existing plan.

          Risk & safety

          • Leave every risk checkbox unchecked and the note still generates: "No risk or safety concerns identified this session." That's a real clinical claim — only let it stand if it's true.
          • If something concerning happened, check "Concerns present" and write it out — a checkbox alone says nothing about what occurred.

          Common Individual Note mistakes, at a glance

          Every pitfall from this module, gathered in one place.

          Scope
          Writing it like a therapy transcript
          Documenting everything discussed, every disclosure, every backstory detail. This is part of the discoverable legal record — it can be subpoenaed and used against the client. Document progress toward the objective, not the session's full emotional content.
          Reference format
          Not double-checking the section on similarly-named goals
          The note now includes the section automatically ("Objective 1A of Goal A of Section C"), which fixes the old ambiguity — but if a client has goals with the same letter in more than one section, it's still worth a glance to confirm the right one generated.
          Session types
          Using "Reviewing" for a first meeting
          A first session has nothing to review yet. Use First Meeting/Initial ITP so the note doesn't claim progress that hasn't happened.
          Session description
          Letting the auto-text stand alone
          The generated line just says an objective was reviewed — your free text is what proves what actually happened in the room.
          Response
          Marking an objective "Completed" without evidence
          The checkbox generates a clean sentence either way. Only check it when your progress notes actually back it up.
          Risk & safety
          Leaving concerns unchecked out of habit
          The default line reassures a reviewer nothing is wrong. If something is, "Concerns present" plus real detail is the only way that shows up.
          Every note
          A note with no goal or objective reference at all
          Without a reference, the note reads as an unstructured conversation, not treatment toward a documented plan.

          Frequently asked questions

          The questions staff actually ask about writing Individual Notes, answered directly. Click each one.

          + Can I include everything the client told me in session?
          No — and this matters more than it might seem. A progress note is not a therapy note. It documents progress toward a specific objective, not a transcript of the session. These notes are part of the legal medical record and can be subpoenaed in court proceedings — a note full of extensive personal disclosures can end up used against the client, not for them. If something is clinically relevant, acknowledge it briefly and connect it to the objective; don't reproduce the disclosure itself.
          + What if a session touched on more than one goal?
          Add a row for each objective you reviewed or assigned — the tool supports multiple. Reference each one by its own goal letter and objective number.
          + Does selecting "Motivational Interviewing" or "CBT" write anything into the note?
          Not directly — those selections don't currently generate their own sentences. Pick whichever technique the session actually centered on for your own record; if it mattered clinically, say so in your free text too.
          + What if nothing eventful happened this session?
          Same rule as everywhere else in this training: "nothing eventful" is still information, if you say what it means. Client attended, engaged with the assigned objective, no new concerns — that's a complete, honest note.
          + Do I have to assign a new objective at the end of every session?
          No. If the client is still actively working the same objective, leave "Assigned Next" pointed at it, or blank if genuinely nothing changed. Don't invent a new one just to fill the field.
          + Why does Crisis Intervention hide the ITP reference fields entirely?
          A crisis note is documenting an acute event, not routine progress on the existing plan — the tool's own auto-text says as much: the client's original ITP goals aren't a reflection of the crisis note.
